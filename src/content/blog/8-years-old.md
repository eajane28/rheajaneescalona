Social media is a double-edged sword. On one hand, it allows us to connect with minds across the globe. On the other, it endlessly feeds our existential crises, traps us in a loop of comparison, and forces us to measure our lives against impossible ideals. As a chronically online Gen Z-er now navigating my mid-20s, I feel this friction daily. I often find myself staring blankly with a realization: **How is it that I felt like I had my life completely figured out in my teens, but now in my 20s, I feel entirely lost?** As it turns out, the answer doesn't lie in looking forward. It lies in taking a moment to look back on what matters. According to developmental psychology research compiled by the National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine, the age of 8 is a massive cognitive milestone. It is the exact threshold where we develop a distinct internal identity before the crushing weight of adolescent peer pressure, financial panic, or ego takes over. Your 8-year-old self represents the most sophisticated, uncorrupted version of you. It is your baseline blueprint. Because of this, I decided to treat this blog as a journey of self-discovery, starting with an audit of my own childhood.

Interestingly, out of all my childhood memories, my recollections of being eight years old are sparse. But when I dug deep into the years 2009 and 2010, a few distinct fragments surfaced that told me everything I needed to know about who I am at my core.
- **The Drive for Excellence**: This was the year I placed second overall in my grade. It was already an achievement for me, but my mama wasn't happy with it at all. While I didn't personally see a problem with second place, her reaction sparked something in me. I promised myself right then and there that I would be better, simply because I wanted to make her proud.
- **The Thirst for Knowledge**: I remembered being fond with books. Our school library which is an abandoned room right next to our classroom was a place I constantly romanticized. I really wanted to get inside, driven by a raw curiosity to explore what was hidden on those dusty shelves.
- **The Value of Fair Leadership**: I vividly remember my Grade 3 teacher. She was strict given her profession as a teacher who genuinely want us to learn, but an incredibly generous person. She would often share her food with us if any of us don't have any. If we did our chores and cleaned the classroom, she would give us food for doing the work. She taught me early on that hard work deserves to be rewarded, and that firm boundaries can coexist with kindness.
- **The Need for Stillness**: My favorite afternoons were spent chilling with my classmates and friends under the shade of a Talisay tree. We would eat our lunch there, rest, and just exist.

Looking back at those memories, life felt remarkably simple. Yet, beneath that simplicity lay the exact map of the adult I am trying to become today. My 8-year-old self didn't want a chaotic, high-stress life to impress strangers online. Through those memories, I realized my true desire for fulfillment: **I want a peaceful, simple day-to-day life, while simultaneously holding a fierce, quiet ambition to get better in my career.** If you are currently feeling lost in your 20s, take a moment for yourself alone and look back at your 8-year-old self, and ask: **What did I care about before the world told me what to care about?** Your blueprint is already written. You just have to remember it.

Thanks for taking the time to read! I hope this reminds you to pause for a while and remember what you want before everyone else does.
